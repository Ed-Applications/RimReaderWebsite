Adjust toe safely

Rim Reader tells you which way to turn the front of each tire and how far. The wrenching is yours, so do it safely.

Before you pick up a wrench
  • Work on firm, level ground with the engine off, the parking brake on and the wheels chocked.
  • Never get under, or reach under, a vehicle held up only by a jack. Use rated jack stands.
  • Inspect the tie-rod ends, ball joints, bushings and tires first. Do not adjust or reuse worn, bent or damaged parts.
  • Follow the vehicle maker’s procedure and specifications.

In and out

In means the tire’s front toward the car’s middle. Out means the tire’s front away from the middle.

{{lesson:Toe In and Out}}

Step by step

  1. Check the confidence. If it is Low, measure again before adjusting.
  2. Hold the steering wheel straight while you adjust. Lock it if you can.
  3. Turn the tie rod a little on the wheel the app names. Whether lengthening or shortening the rod turns the tire in or out depends on the car, so turn it a little, measure, and watch the number move the right way.
  4. Tighten the jam nuts to the maker’s torque, and lug nuts and other fasteners too if you touched them.
  5. Recheck the wheel. On the result, long-press the wheel you adjusted. The app reads just that wheel, in about a minute, and shows its new toe beside the old one. Recheck one wheel
  6. When you’re done adjusting, measure again with a full alignment and read the new numbers. With a Mobile Mechanic plan, Garage & History also shows before and after: tick both runs and tap Compare. A change too small to be real shows as no measurable change.
  7. Check the steering moves freely from lock to lock and the steering wheel is centered.
  8. Take the phone and the bracket off before the car moves.
  9. Test-drive slowly in a safe, empty place. If the car pulls, wanders, vibrates or makes noise, stop and have it inspected.

Recheck one wheel

After a full alignment you don’t need another lap to see what an adjustment did. On the result, or on the reading in History, long-press the wheel you adjusted, tap Toe Recheck, tick any other wheel you adjusted, and tap Start. The app reads that wheel against the front door on the same side: four holds at the door and four at the wheel, about a minute. Keep the front windows up and the doors shut, and don’t lean on the car or roll it.

{{lesson:Recheck a Wheel}}

Adjust one wheel at a time, rear wheels first, and recheck after each. To center the steering wheel, turn both front tie rods the same amount in opposite directions, then recheck both front wheels together. When you’re done, run a full alignment to confirm. Recheck comes with every plan, starting with DIYer. Adjusted camber? Tap Camber Recheck instead: Recheck camber.

{{more:What a recheck needs|recheck-needs}}

The alignment it starts from must be the vehicle’s newest, under 12 hours old, rated High or Medium, and taken with the bracket calibration the phone has now. Without Thrust ticked in that alignment, a front wheel is read against the rear wheel on its side and a rear wheel against the other rear wheel; the app asks whether that wheel was adjusted, and if it was, a new alignment is needed. A front recheck reads only that front wheel: if the steering wheel moved while you worked, recheck the other front wheel too.
